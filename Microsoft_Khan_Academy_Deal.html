01 / The deal · May 2024

Microsoft supplied AI infrastructure so Khan Academy could make its teacher tools free across the U.S.

Microsoft brought cloud infrastructure and AI technology. Khan Academy brought teaching tools and educational content. Their partnership combined free teacher access, exploratory math AI development, and planned content integration. [1] [2]

MICROSOFT ANNOUNCEMENT · MAY 21, 2024KHAN ACADEMY POST · MAY 20, 2024

Khan Academy’s page displays May 20, 2024 and a March 20, 2025 update. Microsoft dates the free U.S. launch to May 21, 2024. This analysis concerns the 2024 agreement.

02 / The trade · Two parties

What each side gave, received, and why.

Microsoft

Technology provider · Azure, Phi-3, Copilot, Teams

What it gave

  • Donated access to AI infrastructure, enabling free U.S. teacher access to Khanmigo. [2]
  • Azure OpenAI Service and collaboration on exploratory Phi-3 math tutoring. [1]

What it received

  • Educational math content, worked answers, feedback, and benchmarking data for model development. [1]
  • A collaboration to integrate more Khan Academy content into Copilot and Teams for Education. [2]
On the record · Exact excerpts

Content contribution:

“ongoing feedback, and benchmarking data to evaluate performance.”

Microsoft Education Team · May 21, 2024 · Microsoft Education Blog [1]

“We’re thrilled to tackle these important challenges with Khan Academy”

Deirdre Quarnstrom · Microsoft Education Product Vice President · May 21, 2024 · Microsoft Education Blog [1]

The surrounding passage discusses accessibility and reliability in AI math tutoring. The excerpt is not a disclosure of every motive.

I think working with an established education nonprofit gives Microsoft a useful setting to improve its AI and make its education products more relevant to teachers.

My interpretation; not a quotation or a verified statement of Microsoft’s motive.

Khan Academy

Education nonprofit · Khanmigo and learning content

What it gave

  • Its existing teacher tools and educational library. [2]
  • Math questions, worked answers, feedback, and evaluation data for exploratory AI work. [1]

What it received

  • Infrastructure support to offer Khanmigo for Teachers free to U.S. teachers. [2]
  • Planned wider distribution of its content through Microsoft education products. [2]

Evidence limits: the announcements do not specify a dollar value for the donation or complete contractual terms. Microsoft says Khan Academy user data will not train the model. Free teacher access does not establish free student access. Planned integrations and exploratory development are not proof of completed results. [1–3]

03 / The shape

A technology and content collaboration.

MY CLASSIFICATION · ANALYSIS

I would classify this as a strategic collaboration combining infrastructure support, AI development, and content distribution. Microsoft supplies computing capabilities; Khan Academy supplies educational expertise and content. The public announcements do not establish the agreement’s legal classification, exclusivity, or full licensing terms.

04 / My call

The strongest trade is complementary expertise.

My judgment: the deal makes sense because each organization contributes something the other would take time to build. As an Advanced Algebra teacher with a computer science background, I would judge its success by accurate math explanations and useful time savings that leave teachers more time to support students.
